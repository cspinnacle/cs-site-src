Most successful cyberattacks don't rely on clever hacking — they rely on someone clicking the wrong link. Here's what actually helps.

## 1. Passwords: length beats complexity

A long, memorable phrase ("purple-elephant-trampoline-42") is harder to crack than a short, complex-looking one ("P@ss1!"). A password manager makes this painless.

## 2. Phishing: slow down before you click

Phishing emails and texts try to create urgency — "your account will be suspended!" The habit that stops most of them: pause, check the sender's actual address, and go to the site directly instead of clicking the link.

## 3. Two-factor authentication

Turning on two-factor authentication (a code sent to your phone, in addition to your password) blocks the vast majority of account takeovers, even if a password leaks.

## Where this shows up in class

Cybersecurity fundamentals appear in 6th grade's Intro to the Internet module and again, in more depth, in 9th grade's Cybersecurity and Global Impacts unit.
