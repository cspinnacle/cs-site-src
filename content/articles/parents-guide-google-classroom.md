Google Classroom doesn't have a separate "parent login" the way some school systems do — but there are still a few real ways to stay in the loop without needing your student's password.

![Illustration of a parent and child spending quality time together](https://cdn.undraw.co/illustration/quality-time_h2b9.svg)

## Option 1: Guardian email summaries (the easiest way)

Google Classroom has a built-in feature called **guardian summaries** — automated emails sent directly to a parent or guardian's own email address, with no login required. They typically include:

- Missing work
- Upcoming assignments
- Recent class announcements

You can request these be turned on by emailing the teacher and asking to be added as a guardian on the class. Once set up, the summaries arrive on their own schedule (daily or weekly) straight to your inbox.

## Option 2: Ask your student to show you their view

Since there's no independent parent dashboard, the most complete picture still comes from looking at the class with your student — the **Stream** tab for announcements, and **Classwork** for assignments, due dates, and turned-in status. Five minutes once a week is usually enough to catch anything that's slipping.

## Google Classroom vs. QuickSchools — what's the difference?

It's easy to conflate the two, but they serve different purposes:

| | Google Classroom | QuickSchools |
|---|---|---|
| What it shows | Day-to-day assignments, materials, announcements | Official grades and attendance record |
| Who has a login | Students (and guardians, if invited) | Parents, via office-issued credentials |
| Source of truth for report cards | No | Yes |

If you only have time for one, QuickSchools is the official record — but Classroom is where you'll catch a missing assignment early enough to actually fix it.

## The one-sentence version

You don't need your child's password to stay informed — ask for guardian email summaries, and check in on their Classwork tab together once in a while.
