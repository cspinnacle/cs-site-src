Google Classroom is where day-to-day assignments and materials live, but QuickSchools is Pinnacle Academy's official Student Information System (SIS) — it's the record that actually counts for report cards and attendance. Here's how to read it.

![Illustration of a person reviewing a stack of graded papers](https://cdn.undraw.co/illustration/grading-papers_7fpu.svg)

## Getting access

Login credentials are distributed by the school office at the start of the year — not by individual teachers. If you don't have a login yet, or you're locked out, contact the office directly rather than your child's teacher; they don't control account access.

## Finding grades

Once logged in, look for the **Gradebook** or **Grades** section. It's organized by course, so Computer Science will show up as its own entry alongside your student's other classes. Inside a course, you'll typically see:

- Individual assignment scores, most recent first
- An overall course grade, calculated from those scores
- Sometimes a breakdown by category (for example, Projects vs. Classwork), if the teacher has set categories up

## Finding attendance

The **Attendance** tab shows a daily record — present, absent, or tardy — for each class period. This is the official record the school uses for attendance policy purposes, separate from anything shown in Google Classroom.

## A quick note on timing

Grades in QuickSchools are usually updated in batches, not the instant something is graded in Classroom — so don't be surprised if there's a short lag between a returned assignment and an updated overall grade. If a specific score looks wrong or missing after a few days, that's worth a message to the teacher directly.

## Who to contact

- **Can't log in / forgot password** → the school office
- **A specific grade looks wrong** → the subject teacher
- **General questions about how grading works for a class** → check that class's page on this site, or ask the teacher

## The one-sentence version

Google Classroom shows the day-to-day work; QuickSchools is the official record of grades and attendance — when in doubt about what "really counts," QuickSchools is the source of truth.
